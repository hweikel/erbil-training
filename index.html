Rigorous definitions are challenging — but they have to come before policy

  • Major cultural and academic institutions in the US & UK are facing public crises over AI policies
  • Nobody involved can seem to agree what constitutes improper usage or even what AI is

Building a curriculum is the most impactful approach

  • The current academic response in the US & UK is retreat — blue book exams, mirrors during zoom exams, no homework
  • Thesis of curriculum: raising students to understand tools will lead to better use (and potentially less use overall as a result)
  • Western schools may have gotten these tools earlier, but CIS is significantly ahead by exploring a targeted curriculum

Key takeaways

  • Models are non-deterministic
  • Models make predictions based on limited understandings of the world provided by training data
  • The model is only one part of the tool
  • Modern AI tools are incredibly complex and not fully understood

AI foundations

AI failure modes

AI failure modes

Non-determinism

  • Inputs do not produce consistent outputs

AI failure modes

Sycophancy

  • Models tend to produce responses that flatter and deceive human users

AI failure modes

Bland answers

  • Statistical tools are bad at extremes: they “regress to the mean” of their data by design.

AI failure modes

Modality blindspots

  • Models reason about the world through statistical representations of a specific data modality
  • LLM calculations and spatial reasoning are structurally flawed as a result

AI failure modes

Biased training data

  • All data is incomplete and thus biased
  • Models inherit and amplify biases

AI failure modes

AI detectors don’t work

  • False positives are constant and hugely damaging to students’ confidence
  • English Second Language learners suffer the most
  • Alternatives: clear standards, grade the process not the output

AI failure modes

Safety risks

  • Sycophancy and long conversations create psychological danger
  • Consumer chatbot protections do not work
  • Almost any mainstream AI tool can be broken with prompt injection

AI ethics & student agency

“Ethics as a practice”

  • Context: AI use policies alone don’t work — top western schools struggle to implement rules-based systems, harming students and teachers in the process
  • Goal: empower students to create their own relationship with all tools by integrating ethics and agency into the curriculum
  • Method: regular ethics discussions feeding into personal AI use constitutions

AI ethics & student agency

Risks & solutions

Problem

Cognitive debt & developing minds

  • Using LLMs while completing traditional academic or creative tasks fundamentally changes brain activation, reducing neural connectivity and sense of ownership
  • Other tools are not analogous: comparisons to search engines & word processors
  • Too early to know the long-term impacts on development

Source: Kosmyna, N., Hauptmann, E., Yuan, Y.T., Situ, J., Liao, X.-H., Beresnitzky, A.V., Braunstein, I., & Maes, P. (2025). “Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task.” MIT Media Lab. arXiv:2506.08872. · brainonllm.com

Solution

Cognitive debt & developing minds

  • Staged tool use
  • Mastery before amplification — “force multiplier”
Problem

The overcommitment trap

  • Current climate around productivity, self help, and ambition — big goals end up backing students into corners to produce
  • The most ambitious students tend to be hurt the most in periods of early tool adoption
Solution

The overcommitment trap

  • Assess process, inputs, and understanding rather than outputs
  • Personal AI use constitution
Problem

Discipline & fairness

  • AI use policies are unclear, students get in trouble often unfairly
Solution

Discipline & fairness

  • Make policies clearer
  • Enable success rather than punishing failure

AI use constitutions

What is it?

  • A personal & actionable set of principles around AI usage
  • A living document — updated regularly to reflect changes in personal values, current projects, and available resources
  • Shifts the discussion from discipline (in both senses of the word) to growth & development
  • Centers student agency
